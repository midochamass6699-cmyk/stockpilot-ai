Reports use historical company facts filed with the U.S. SEC. Coverage varies by company and accounting standard.

What information is included?

Where reported in SEC company facts, the report displays revenue, gross profit, operating income, net income, assets, liabilities, shareholders’ equity, cash balances and cash flows. It shows up to three annual periods where available and links to SEC filings.

This first data connection focuses on U.S. SEC registrants and US-GAAP facts. Some issuers, foreign reporting standards or individual metrics may not be covered. Missing data is labelled rather than estimated.

How to read the report

Compare matching fiscal periods and note whether a value is a year-end balance or a full-year result. Check the linked filing for definitions, accounting policies, restatements and additional context. Financial statement values are historical disclosures, not live share prices.

Educational use: this report organizes published information. It is not a forecast, personal financial advice, or a recommendation to buy or sell any security.

Frequently asked questions

Does it recognize every company?

No. The first version searches companies in the SEC's public company ticker list. Coverage and financial facts depend on whether the company files data with the SEC and whether the relevant facts are available in the supported taxonomy.

Are the figures real?

The figures are retrieved from SEC EDGAR company facts. Each report shows reporting dates and links to SEC filings so readers can check the original disclosures.

Is this live market data?

No. This report covers historical financial statements, not live or delayed stock quotes.

How to read generated financial ratios

A generated report may combine figures copied from company filings with calculations performed by the tool. These are different evidence types. Reported revenue, cash and debt should be traceable to a filing and a specific period. A margin, growth rate or leverage ratio should be treated as a calculation whose formula and inputs can be checked.

Review these details before relying on a result

  • Confirm the legal issuer, filing type, fiscal year and period end date.
  • Check whether values are presented in dollars, thousands or millions.
  • Look for restatements, discontinued operations and one-time items.
  • Check whether a ratio uses average or period-end balances.
  • Compare the calculated result with the company's own explanation and prior periods.

Missing data should remain missing; it should not be silently replaced with zero. A ratio is a starting point for questions, not a complete assessment of a company's financial condition.
