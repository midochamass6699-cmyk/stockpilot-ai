Understand the U.S. financial markets.

A practical, independent introduction to the structure of U.S. markets, common account terminology, investor protections, costs and financial risk. Designed to help readers understand the system—not to tell them what to buy or sell.

Educational information only. This page is not investment, tax or legal advice. It does not provide stock recommendations, trading instructions or endorsements of financial products.

How the U.S. markets are organized

The United States has several major securities markets. A company's listing venue, trading volume, share price and business fundamentals are different concepts; none alone establishes whether an investment is appropriate.

New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)

A major U.S. stock exchange with listings across many industries and company sizes.

Nasdaq

A major electronic marketplace known for technology companies as well as businesses from many other sectors.

NYSE American and OTC markets

Other U.S. market venues have different listing standards, disclosure practices and liquidity characteristics. Lower-priced shares can carry additional risks.

Market indexes

Indexes such as the S&P 500, Dow Jones Industrial Average and Nasdaq Composite summarize different groups of securities using different methodologies.

Common U.S. account terminology

Account names describe legal and tax arrangements. Eligibility, contribution rules, withdrawal treatment and employer-plan features can differ substantially.

Account typeGeneral purposeImportant distinction
401(k)Employer-sponsored retirement savings plan.Plan rules, employer contributions, fees and investment choices vary by employer.
Traditional IRAIndividual retirement account with tax treatment governed by U.S. rules.Deductibility and withdrawal taxation depend on eligibility and circumstances.
Roth IRAIndividual retirement account funded with after-tax contributions.Income limits, contribution rules and qualified-withdrawal conditions apply.
Taxable brokerage accountNon-retirement investment account.Interest, dividends, realized gains and losses may have tax consequences.
529 planTax-advantaged account intended for qualified education expenses.State rules, qualified expenses and plan features differ.

These are broad descriptions, not eligibility guidance. Consult current IRS materials and the plan documents that apply to a particular account.

U.S. market oversight and investor information

SEC

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission oversees federal securities laws, public-company disclosures and key parts of the securities markets. SEC investor resources.

FINRA

The Financial Industry Regulatory Authority is a self-regulatory organization overseeing broker-dealers and providing investor education tools. FINRA investor resources.

Investor.gov

The SEC's investor education site explains investing basics, fees, fraud awareness and questions to consider. Visit Investor.gov.

SIPC

SIPC protection has a limited purpose and is not insurance against market losses or a guarantee that an investment will retain its value. SIPC information for investors.

Costs, uncertainty and risk

Costs are more than a headline commission

Financial products and accounts can involve expense ratios, advisory charges, spreads, transaction fees, account fees, transfer fees and tax consequences. The relevant costs depend on the product and account terms.

Market prices can move in either direction

Market values fluctuate. Diversification can reduce some concentration risks, but it cannot eliminate market risk or guarantee a positive outcome. Past performance does not establish future results.

Be cautious with leverage and complex products

Borrowing to invest, derivatives and other complex products can magnify losses and introduce obligations that are difficult to understand. Complexity does not make a product suitable or safer.

Recognize misleading claims

Be skeptical of guaranteed-return claims, pressure to act immediately, unsolicited investment offers and requests to send money through unusual payment methods. Verify firms and individuals through official regulatory resources.
