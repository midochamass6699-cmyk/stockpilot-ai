Cash vs. Margin Brokerage Accounts in the United States

A U.S. cash account and a margin account differ in whether the brokerage firm lends money to the customer. Margin is a loan arrangement with additional costs and risks—not simply a different screen setting.

Cash account

You pay the full amount for securities purchased. You do not borrow from the brokerage firm to fund those purchases. Settlement and account rules still apply.

Margin account

The brokerage firm may lend money using eligible securities and cash in the account as collateral. Interest applies, and the firm can impose maintenance requirements.

Costs and account conditions

Margin interest reduces investment returns. Rates, calculation methods, eligible collateral and maintenance requirements vary by firm and may change. Read the margin agreement and current fee schedule carefully.

Margin calls and liquidation

If account equity falls below the firm's maintenance requirement, the firm may require additional funds or securities. Depending on the agreement and applicable rules, the firm may sell securities—sometimes without first contacting the customer—to address a shortfall. Losses can exceed the cash initially contributed.

U.S. rules and official explanation

Federal Reserve, FINRA and exchange rules apply, while brokerage firms may set stricter house requirements. The SEC's Investor.gov explains minimum margin, initial margin, maintenance margin, interest and liquidation risks.
