SK hynix and the AI Memory Boom: HBM, Contracts and the 2027 Outlook

AI processors attract attention, but they cannot deliver their full performance without fast, high-capacity memory. SK hynix is one of the central suppliers in that market. Here is what the company has disclosed, how its competitors compare, and what could matter as 2027 begins.

Published November 27, 2026 · StockPilot AI Research · Updated for information available on the publication date

The central question for 2027 is not simply whether demand for artificial intelligence will grow. It is whether memory manufacturers can deliver enough advanced memory, at acceptable yields and prices, to support the next generation of AI systems. SK hynix is positioned to benefit from that demand, particularly through high-bandwidth memory (HBM), but strong industry conditions do not make its share price predictable or remove the risks of a cyclical semiconductor market.

Why AI systems need high-bandwidth memory

Training and running large AI models requires processors to move enormous quantities of data between compute units and memory. If the processor waits for data, expensive computing capacity is underused. HBM addresses this bottleneck by stacking DRAM dies vertically and connecting them with very wide, high-speed interfaces. The result is much greater memory bandwidth in a compact package than conventional memory can provide.

HBM is integrated closely with AI accelerators. It is not interchangeable with a standard PC memory module: packaging, thermal management, power delivery, signal integrity and manufacturing yields all matter. As models become larger, context windows lengthen and AI services handle more simultaneous users, the amount and speed of memory available to each accelerator become increasingly important.

AI infrastructure also needs conventional server DRAM and solid-state storage. DRAM supports system memory and data handling; NAND-based SSDs store model files, datasets, checkpoints and frequently accessed information. HBM is the high-bandwidth layer closest to the accelerator, while DRAM and SSDs serve different parts of the system. Growth in AI infrastructure can therefore lift demand across several memory categories, although not at identical rates.

What SK hynix has actually secured

SK hynix's October 28, 2026 third-quarter results provide the clearest company-level evidence. The company reported revenue of 104.6155 trillion won and operating profit of 83.3338 trillion won for the quarter. It said HBM4 and other high-value server products expanded, with HBM4 expected to become a mainstream product from the fourth quarter of 2026.

More important for visibility, SK hynix said it had signed long-term agreements with 14 major customers, including global cloud-service providers. It said the agreements typically run for five years and that the capacity covered represented more than 50% of its total production capacity for 2027 and 2028. The company described a pricing structure that reflects market conditions within agreed ranges. This can help customers secure supply and help SK hynix plan capacity, but it does not mean every future shipment or revenue figure is guaranteed.

Industry reporting in November described customers seeking multiyear supply arrangements as they compete for memory. The same reporting noted that contract volumes and delivery periods can still depend on demand and business conditions. Public disclosures do not provide the full customer-by-customer terms, price floors, cancellation rights or penalties. It would be inaccurate to claim that specific undisclosed deals with named AI companies have been signed.

Capacity expansion and storage opportunities

SK hynix is investing to expand production and advanced packaging. Its U.S. Indiana project is a major part of its longer-term supply-chain plan; Reuters reported that volume production of next-generation HBM4E at the facility is planned for the third quarter of 2029. That timing is important: it is a strategic expansion, not a source of significant new output for the first quarter of 2027.

For early 2027, the more immediate variables are the ramp of existing facilities, HBM4 yields, packaging capacity, customer qualification and the mix of products shipped. NAND storage is another opportunity through SK hynix and its Solidigm business, particularly enterprise SSDs used in data centres. But SSD demand and HBM demand should not be treated as the same market: they have different competitors, pricing cycles and supply constraints.

How the competitors compare

  • Samsung Electronics: A broad competitor across DRAM, HBM and NAND. Samsung has the scale and product breadth to compete for AI, server, mobile and storage demand. Its ability to qualify and ramp advanced HBM products is a key variable. Its own long-term customer agreements also show that competition is not limited to SK hynix.
  • Micron Technology: A major U.S. DRAM and NAND supplier expanding its HBM business. Micron's public commentary has pointed to tight memory supply-demand conditions in 2027 and 2028 and substantial customer commitments. Its progress in HBM qualification, manufacturing yields and capacity additions will influence competitive supply.
  • Western Digital and Seagate: Important storage companies, especially in hard-disk drives. They are relevant to the broader data-centre storage buildout, but they are not direct HBM manufacturers and should not be compared with SK hynix as if they sell the same product.

SK hynix's competitive case rests on its established HBM position, customer relationships, product execution and ability to increase high-value output. Samsung's breadth and Micron's expanding HBM presence create real competition. Market share can change as customers qualify multiple suppliers and as product generations evolve.

What could shape December 2026 through March 2027?

There is no reliable public basis for a precise SK hynix share-price forecast for the second week of December, January, February or March. A share price can move sharply even when the business outlook remains positive, because investors continually adjust expectations for earnings, valuation, currency movements, interest rates and semiconductor-cycle risk.

For the second week of December, investors and researchers can monitor any new company guidance, customer announcements, HBM qualification updates and broader technology-sector sentiment. During January, attention is likely to turn to year-end results, 2027 capital spending plans and management commentary about HBM4 production and customer demand. February and March may bring further evidence about actual shipments, yields, pricing and the pace of AI data-centre investment. These are monitoring points, not predictions that the stock will rise in those months.

The risks that could change the outlook

The bullish case depends on continued spending by cloud providers and AI developers. If customers delay data-centre projects, reduce accelerator orders or optimize systems to use less memory, demand expectations could reset. Long-term agreements improve visibility but their detailed terms matter; announced capacity coverage is not the same as guaranteed profits. Rapid capacity additions by SK hynix, Samsung and Micron could eventually ease shortages and pressure prices. HBM production also carries technical risks: a slower yield ramp, packaging bottlenecks or a product qualification delay can affect shipments.

Investors should also distinguish company performance from stock performance. Strong earnings may already be reflected in the share price. Valuation, Korean market conditions, the won, global rates and risk appetite can all affect returns independently of quarterly operating results.

Bottom line: SK hynix enters 2027 with significant exposure to AI memory demand, an established HBM business and disclosed long-term agreements covering more than half of its 2027–2028 production capacity. Those facts support a strong industrial position, not a guaranteed share-price gain. The most useful evidence to watch is HBM4 execution, customer commitments becoming shipments, pricing, yields, capacity expansion and whether AI infrastructure spending remains durable.
