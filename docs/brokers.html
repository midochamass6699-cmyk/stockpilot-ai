ONLINE BROKERAGE COMPARISON GUIDE

Online broker comparison: Canada and the United States

Choosing an online broker involves more than finding a headline offer for zero-commission stock trading. This independent online broker comparison brings together key information that Canadian and U.S. investors often want to check before evaluating a brokerage platform: trading commissions, other account costs, customer support by telephone, withdrawal processing times, website and app usability, and public customer-review samples.

People searching for the best online brokers in Canada, the best online brokers in the USA, a commission-free stock trading platform, or a low-fee online brokerage should compare the same features on the same basis. There is no single best broker for everyone. Availability, products, fees and account rules can differ depending on where a customer lives and which legal entity provides the service.

Canadian online brokers

For a Canadian online broker comparison, this page covers independent brokerage platforms including Wealthsimple, Questrade, Qtrade Direct Investing, Interactive Brokers Canada, Webull Canada and moomoo Canada. Check whether the exact Canadian entity accepts residents of your province and which Canadian or U.S. securities it supports.

U.S. online brokers

The U.S. online brokerage comparison includes Robinhood, Interactive Brokers Lite, Firstrade and Webull. U.S. account eligibility is not universal: Canadian residents should not assume they can open a U.S. retail brokerage account or use an offer intended for U.S. customers.

Zero-commission trading and the real cost

A zero-commission broker may still charge currency-conversion costs, options contract fees, market-data fees, regulatory or exchange charges, account fees, wire-transfer fees or other service charges. Compare the full brokerage fee schedule—not only the advertised stock commission—before drawing conclusions about the cheapest online broker.

Withdrawals, processing times and holds

Broker withdrawal speed depends on the withdrawal method, currency, bank, settlement status and account type. A published estimate is not a guarantee that money will arrive instantly. Recent deposits, unsettled trades, identity checks, compliance reviews and fraud-prevention controls can delay or restrict withdrawals. Check deposit holds separately from withdrawal processing times.

Telephone customer service and reviews

When comparing broker customer support, check whether a real telephone channel is available, the hours of operation, whether callbacks are limited, and which issues can be handled by phone. Public review websites can help identify recurring customer-experience themes, but review scores are self-selected, can be global, and do not prove that a brokerage is safe or financially sound.

Online trading platform and mobile app usability

An online trading platform should make it practical to find account information, understand order status, locate statements and contact customer service. Compare the broker website, browser-based trading platform, desktop software and mobile app separately. A simple interface may suit a new user, while advanced charting and order tools can require more learning.

How to compare brokerage fees and account features

Start with the securities you actually want to research, then compare the applicable commission schedule, currency conversion, account-maintenance charges, minimum balances and transfer fees. Confirm whether a zero-dollar commission applies to the specific exchange, security type and order method. A U.S.-listed stock, a Canadian-listed stock, an option contract and a foreign-currency transaction may all have different costs.

Next, compare the broker's funding and withdrawal instructions. Look for the stated processing window, eligible bank-transfer methods, minimum or maximum amounts, fees, account-name matching rules and any temporary holds. Distinguish a broker's processing time from the time required by a receiving bank.

Finally, review customer-service hours, platform demonstrations, mobile accessibility, account statements and regulatory disclosures. This page is a research starting point, not a promise of instant approval or unrestricted access. Every regulated broker must apply the identity and eligibility checks required for the account and jurisdiction.

Online broker comparison FAQs

What is the difference between a zero-commission broker and a free broker?

Zero commission usually refers to the trading commission on specified securities. Other costs may still apply, including currency conversion, options, exchange and regulatory charges, market data, spreads, account services and withdrawals. Read the full fee schedule for the account and market you would use.

Can Canadian residents use every U.S. online broker?

No. A broker may accept only residents of particular countries or states, and some account types or commission plans are restricted to U.S. residents. Confirm the legal entity, country eligibility and account terms directly from the broker's official information.

Which online broker has the fastest withdrawals?

There is no universal fastest broker. Processing times differ by payment method, currency, account type, settlement status and bank. Compare the published estimate for the exact withdrawal method and check whether recent deposits or other transactions are subject to holds.

Do online brokers provide telephone customer support?

Some provide direct phone support, while others use chat, secure messages or limited callback services. Check the broker's current contact page for phone hours, regional support numbers and which account issues can be handled by telephone.

Does a high customer-review score mean a broker is reliable?

No. Review scores are opinions posted by a self-selected group of customers and may combine different products and countries. For regulatory status and disciplinary information, check the relevant securities regulator or registration database for the exact legal entity.

Can a brokerage account be approved instantly with no identity checks?

Do not rely on that expectation. Account opening is subject to identity verification, age, residency and other legal requirements. A broker may request more documents or take additional time to review an application. Strong verification protects accounts and is not a drawback to avoid.
