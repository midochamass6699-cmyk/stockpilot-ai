The connected opportunity

AI is more than software. It's an entire physical economy.

Every new computing workload depends on a chain of suppliers: chips, servers, networking, power delivery, cooling, buildings and skilled operators.

That means the industry story is bigger than any single AI developer. Understanding the supporting layers helps explain where capacity constraints, capital spending and competition may emerge.

✳
01 / COMPUTE

AI & Semiconductors

Semiconductors are the computing foundation: accelerators handle parallel workloads, memory feeds data to processors, and foundries and equipment makers enable advanced chip production.

Compute acceleratorsSpecialized processors used to train and run demanding AI workloads.
Memory & storageHigh-bandwidth memory and storage systems keep large workloads supplied with data.
Manufacturing ecosystemFoundries, chip-design tools, packaging and production equipment.
02 / FACILITIES

Data Centres

Data centres combine real estate, electrical systems, servers, security and operations. Their expansion depends on available power, suitable sites, construction capacity and network access.

Colocation & operatorsFacilities that house servers and connect customers to digital networks.
Server infrastructureRacks, storage, power distribution and high-density computing hardware.
Construction & equipmentElectrical contractors, switchgear, backup systems and facility engineering.
03 / ELECTRICITY

Energy & Power Grids

More electricity demand can put attention on generation, transmission, grid modernization and equipment lead times. The local grid's ability to connect new facilities can matter as much as the amount of energy available.

GenerationUtilities and producers supplying electricity from diverse sources.
Grid equipmentTransformers, switchgear, substations and power-management systems.
Backup & resilienceStorage, backup generation and systems that support reliable operation.
04 / THERMAL

Cooling Systems

Dense computing equipment produces heat. Air handling, liquid cooling, heat exchangers and monitoring systems help keep equipment within operating limits while managing energy and water use.

Liquid coolingDirect-to-chip and other liquid-based approaches for high-density racks.
Thermal equipmentChillers, pumps, heat exchangers and precision cooling systems.
Efficiency controlsMonitoring and controls that optimize temperature and energy use.
05 / CONNECTIVITY

Networking

AI clusters and cloud services move enormous amounts of data. Switches, routers, optical transceivers, fibre and network software connect computing resources across racks and locations.

Switching & routingEquipment that moves traffic efficiently within and between facilities.
Optical technologyFibre, transceivers and photonics for high-bandwidth links.
Network managementSoftware and security that keep complex networks reliable.
06 / AUTOMATION

Robotics & Automation

Robotics combines mechanical systems, sensors, control software and AI. Adoption depends on reliability, safety, integration costs and whether automation solves a real operational problem.

Industrial robotsRobotic arms and systems used in manufacturing and logistics.
Motion & sensingMotors, drives, actuators, machine vision and precision sensors.
Automation softwareControl systems, simulation, fleet coordination and integration.
07 / LIFE SCIENCES

Biotechnology & Research Tools

Scientific progress relies on instruments and platforms for measuring, imaging, sequencing and analysing biological systems. Suppliers may serve many research areas rather than one individual treatment or product.

Research instrumentsLaboratory equipment, analytical instruments and measurement systems.
Sequencing & diagnostics toolsPlatforms that help researchers examine genetic and biological data.
Lab supplies & servicesConsumables, reagents, software and outsourced research services.
08 / APPLICATIONS

AI Platforms & Applications

AI developers create foundation models and tools; software companies integrate them into workflows, products and services. Competition, computing costs, customer adoption and the ability to earn sustainable revenue all matter.

Model developersOrganizations building general-purpose and specialized AI models.
Cloud platformsServices that provide computing, model access and deployment infrastructure.
Industry applicationsSoftware applying AI to healthcare research, manufacturing, finance and other fields.
