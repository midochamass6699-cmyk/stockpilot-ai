Where Should You Invest?

Understand the main investment choices before putting your money to work.

1. Individual Stocks

Buying a stock means purchasing a share of a company. Your investment can increase or decrease in value depending on the company's performance and market conditions.

2. ETFs — Exchange-Traded Funds

An ETF can hold many different investments in one product. It can help diversify your portfolio without buying each company separately.

3. Bonds and Fixed-Income Investments

Bonds are investments that represent money lent to a government or company. They have different risks, interest payments, and maturity dates.

4. Registered Canadian Accounts

Canadians may consider accounts such as the TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA, depending on their eligibility and goals. Each account has different tax rules and contribution limits.

How to Start Investing

A beginner-friendly guide to getting started responsibly.

Step 1 — Define Your Goals Decide what you're investing for and when you may need the money.
Step 2 — Understand Your Risk Learn how much volatility and potential loss you can reasonably tolerate.
Step 3 — Research Your Options Compare stocks, ETFs, fees, diversification, and investment account choices.
Step 4 — Choose a Brokerage Compare fees, available investments, account types, and regulatory protections.
Step 5 — Start Carefully Invest only money you can afford to put at risk. Avoid borrowing money to speculate.
Step 6 — Review Regularly Follow your investments, review your goals, and avoid making decisions based only on headlines.
